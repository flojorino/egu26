Stereo-PIV-based assessment of ADCP performance in turbulent open-channel flow

Florian Zirngibl, Marc Ladwig, Mohammd Tanvir Haque Tuhin,
Daniel Peter Gradzki, Ralph Lindken, Christoph Mudersbach, Reinhard Hinkelmann
Hochschule Bochum (ITF & IWH)  ·  Technische Universität Berlin
DFG-funded Project
KÖNIG – A Calibrated and Virtual Multi-Scale Wave Measurement Laboratory for Flow Metrology

The DFG-funded research initiative KÖNIG at Hochschule Bochum investigates the interaction between fluid flow and ultrasonic waves — a fundamental and underexplored topic across many engineering disciplines. At its core, the project establishes a joint calibrated flow and ultrasound laboratory, develops digital twins of the experimental setup, and deepens core competencies in flow simulation, laser-optical flow measurement, and acoustic flow measurement. These foundations are applied in real-world research projects covering flow measurements in rivers and lakes, ultrasound-based material transport monitoring in pipelines, and flow characterization in geothermal reservoirs. This contribution is part of the river hydraulics application, combining Stereo-PIV and ADCP to validate acoustic discharge measurement in turbulent open-channel flow.

Stereo Particle Image Velocimetry (Stereo-PIV) provides high spatial resolution and detailed insight into flow structures in laboratory open-channel hydraulics. Acoustic Doppler Current Profilers (ADCPs) are widely used for velocity and discharge measurements, offering robust data collection even in sediment-laden and optically challenging environments. Their performance depends on acoustic frequency, flow conditions, and the size and concentration of suspended particles. To evaluate both systems under identical conditions, we conducted simultaneous Stereo-PIV and ADCP measurements in a laboratory flume, with emphasis on acoustically challenging regions.

In this contribution, we present results from measurements conducted in a 16 m open-channel flume with a rectangular cross-section at the Laboratory for Hydraulic Engineering and Hydromechanics at Bochum University of Applied Sciences. The experiments covered Reynolds numbers between Re = 4.6 × 10⁴ and 1.6 × 10⁵ and Froude numbers between Fr = 0.04 and 0.1. The stereoscopic PIV setup consists of two CMOS cameras (2560 × 2160 pixels, 16-bit, LaVision Imager sCMOS) with macro lenses (Nikon Nikkor, T2.0/50 mm) and Scheimpflug adapters. PA12 particles with a mean size of 20 µm were used as tracers, illuminated by a Nd:YAG double-pulse laser (Quantel Evergreen 200, 15 Hz, 200 mJ). Measurements were performed in a cross-sectional plane oriented orthogonal to the side walls and translated in 10 mm increments to assess spatial variations along the channel. ADCP measurements used a Sontek RS5 profiler under clear-water conditions to capture velocity profiles in the fully developed flow region. The analysis focuses on ADCP blanking zones and their impact on mean velocities and turbulence proxies.

The objective is to assess the applicability of an ADCP system for open-channel velocity measurements through validation against Stereo-PIV data. The comparison targets bulk flow characteristics relevant to sediment transport analysis and highlights limitations associated with acoustic sampling near boundaries. The results provide a basis for future experiments with mobile beds, where ADCP measurements will support sediment transport estimation.
